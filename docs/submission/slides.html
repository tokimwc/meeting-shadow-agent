01Meeting Shadow Agent
What it does

A reply, and whose decision it is

After each turn of the other side, a card names what is asked and, by the engineer's own memo, whose call it is, then suggests a reply. Simulated client, TTS voices.

m0 — Implementation owner. Staging validation is mine to agree. Production, dates, scope changes and extra effort need internal approval.
u2“Can you confirm that the fix will go live in production on Friday?”
production on Friday · needs internal approval
“I need to confirm internally about the production release on Friday. I will get back to you.”
cites u2 · m0 — suggestion call 1,339 ms
Outside the memo — needs internal approval Inside it — theirs to agree

The engineer reads it and decides. The agent never speaks or messages anyone in the meeting.

The browser streams straight to AssemblyAI. Cloud Run sees finalized text, never the stream.

Refuse, do not repair

Every claim cites the utterance ids behind it. A suggestion citing an id nobody spoke is rejected outright. The check is that the id exists, not that it supports the reply.

The memo is self-declared

It is the engineer's own statement of what they may agree to, typed before the call. Nothing checks it against the authority their company actually gave them.

04Meeting Shadow Agent
Who it is for · a hypothesis, not yet tested with buyers

The engineer feels it.
The delivery lead is the likely buyer.

Uses it

A Japanese-speaking engineer on English client calls, who follows the request but cannot approve dates, production or scope.

Likely buyer

The delivery lead at a software services firm, who absorbs the unpaid rework when a quick yes becomes a commitment.

Has to allow it

Information security: client audio goes to AssemblyAI, the text and the memo to Gemini.

Price hypothesis: $15 per seat per month. Willingness to pay, usage cost and avoided rework have not been measured.

06Meeting Shadow Agent
What it does not show

Three limits, and the check each needs next

  • Wrong agreementsIn the audio run, 4 of 20 final cards agree to what the memo withholds or the speaker left undefined, and the model's own commitment flag has been wrong once. Next: score every card, not only the last.
  • Old questionWhen the client changes the question after a refusal, the card can keep deferring on the old one. Next: test a fix against a model client that argues back.
  • Not tried liveThe cases were written by the builder, and no engineer has read a card during a live call. Next: mock calls against a memorised line and a memo on paper.

Latency, accent errors, retention and reverted prompts: docs/eval in the repository.
