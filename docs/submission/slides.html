Refuse, do not repair

Every claim cites the utterance ids behind it. A suggestion citing an id nobody spoke is rejected outright — a card with invented evidence is worse than no card.

The memo is not a witness

An open item whose only evidence is m0 is dropped. The memo says what needs approval; it is not evidence that anyone asked for it.

05Meeting Shadow Agent
Where this sits

Evidence grounding is not the differentiator

Four agents in this hackathon already tie their output back to what was said:

  • VerbaTrace AIinterviews, detects incomplete answers, converts the conversation into traceable evidence
  • QuoteReadyturns vague enquiries into reviewable drafts with transcript evidence and follow-up gaps
  • Voice Action Gaterefuses any irreversible action whose arguments cannot be traced to the user's words
  • Saakshispeaks up the moment a customer is about to be misled, and certifies consent
Each of them produces something addressed to the conversation — a question put to the other party, a record, a blocked call. This one produces something addressed to the user: the sentence they might say next, shaped by a boundary they declared beforehand, to be said or discarded. The closest thing outside this hackathon is Cluely, which also listens without joining; what it does not do is take a declared authority boundary as the input that shapes the reply.

Stated plainly: neither evidence grounding nor latency is a moat, several of these keep a human in the loop too, and the memo is what the user says their authority is — not what their employer granted. That the memo changes the reply is the next thing to measure, and it is not measured yet.

06Meeting Shadow Agent
What it does not show

The limits

  • Not a studyA developer-authored test suite. Scenarios written by the person building the agent do not generalise to real meetings.
  • Zero is not proofZero failures in twenty trials leaves a one-sided 95% upper bound near 14% — and only for independent trials, which cases written by the builder are not.
  • Self-graded, and wrong onceThe commitment flag is a field the model fills in about its own output. One suggestion agreed to a scope change the memo withholds and reported that it had not; the earlier claim of zero dangerous commitments was withdrawn.
  • One run is one drawSingle-run scores moved between runs of the same prompt by more than most prompts differed from each other. Only the repeated measurements are claims.
  • Wrong card mid-requestA card is rendered for every speaker turn. The final decision is usually right; the one on screen halfway through sometimes agrees to the request.
  • Not end-to-end1.9 s is end of speech → suggestion generated, measured in-process. Card-on-screen is not measured.
  • No outcome claimNothing here shows a real over-commitment was prevented — only that the cards arrive in time and the safety checks hold on the inputs tried.
  • Accent costNon-native speech degrades the transcript on exactly the words a decision turns on. One human take reached the model as “Oh. End of day. Cause visit.”
  • Not zero retentionAssemblyAI offers a TTL. Billing and log metadata outlive it.

Prompt changes that made the suggestions worse were reverted, and every run is in the repository — including the first memo ablation, which found the memo did not decide, and is why the output contract was rewritten.
